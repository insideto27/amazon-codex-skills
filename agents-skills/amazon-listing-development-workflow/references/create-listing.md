# Create Mode

Create the best iterable listing from currently available evidence when the publication decision permits drafting. Do not wait for an ideal market dataset; do stop publish-ready work when product or regulatory facts require it.

## Workflow

1. Identify marketplace, language, brand, product/SKU, category, target variant, and lifecycle stage.
2. Build the initial product-fact and claim ledgers. Record ordinary unknowns without blocking work.
3. For a new product link, invoke `amazon-listing-regulatory-review` after establishing the actual function and target marketplace. Obtain its publication decision before preparing publish-ready wording. If product function is unclear or the decision is `HOLD` or `BLOCK`, deliver the review record and evidence needs without a copy-ready listing.
4. Select direct competitors by shared function and purchase intent. Similar appearance or competitor availability alone is insufficient.
5. Extract available competitor titles, bullets, descriptions, A+, images, price position, reviews, Q&A, and reverse-search keywords.
6. Extract VOC: desired outcomes, pain points, objections, misconceptions, and customer wording.
7. Normalize and cluster keywords into category, function, scenario, audience, compatibility, pain-point, and attribute groups.
8. Establish a provisional positioning: core purchase reason, target user, differentiation, and claim boundaries.
9. Allocate keywords deliberately across title, bullets, description, backend terms, and optional A+. Avoid mechanical repetition.
10. Draft only within the publication decision. Use V0.x for a gated pre-release draft and V1.0 only for a publishable first version.
11. Review the exact final wording and any available cross-surface artifacts against the decision. Deliver copy-ready text only for `PASS`; isolate unverified fields and release evidence for `CONDITIONAL PASS`.
12. Produce bilingual correspondence where copy exists, evidence rationale, assumptions, and future validation metrics.

## Completion standard

The version is complete when its deliverable matches the publication decision, contains no known factual contradiction or unsafe publish-ready claim, and states the evidence still needed. Complete market data is not required.

## Sparse-data behavior

- No advertising or rank data: use competitor keywords, category language, VOC, and semantic relevance.
- Few competitors: use the confirmed direct set and label sample limits in the explanation table.
- Unknown specification: omit the number and emphasize verified function or use outcome.
- Unclear audience: derive a provisional audience from function, purchase scenarios, and VOC; define later validation metrics.
- Weak keyword metrics: triangulate competitor recurrence, reverse-search data, autocomplete, and semantic relevance.
